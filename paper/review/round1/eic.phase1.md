D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer): This dimension concerns whether substantive claims agree with the current evidence base, whether earlier work is represented fairly and accurately, and whether domain terms and reported results are free of factual error. It is outside my scoring remit; editorially I rely on the domain reviewer's judgement and watch for conflicts with other seats.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high priority, owned by the perspective reviewer): This dimension asks whether framing, definitions and implications can be followed by readers from neighbouring fields, and whether any claims that cross disciplinary lines are backed by evidence. It is not mine to score; I note it because a block on a high-priority dimension changes the decision path.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal priority, owned by me): This dimension covers how the manuscript is organised, how clearly it is written, whether figures and tables communicate well, and whether it follows the conventions expected of its article type. Because no target venue has been confirmed, I will judge conventions against general field-level reporting norms rather than any specific venue's author guidelines.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by me): This dimension asks whether the work suits its intended venue and offers an original, significant contribution for that readership. With no author-confirmed review target, I cannot bind to venue criteria and will make no venue-alignment judgement; my assessment is limited to originality, significance and whether the contribution is clearly stated and defensibly bounded for a general readership in the stated field.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: Logical section order matching the article type, a clear statement of aims early on, consistent terminology and abbreviations, tables and figures that stand alone with complete labels and units, numbers in text that match tables, and completed reporting checklist items wherever a reporting guideline is claimed.
what_triggers_block: Exposition so disorganised or internally inconsistent that a reader cannot reliably locate the aims, methods and main results, or tables and figures that contradict the text on key quantities.
what_triggers_warn: Localised clarity problems such as undefined abbreviations, redundant or overlong sections, incomplete figure or table labelling, or claimed reporting guideline items that are missing or hard to find, none of which prevents understanding of the core findings.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution
dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: An explicit statement of what is new relative to prior work, a clearly identified gap, significance argued from evidence rather than asserted, conclusions and implications confined to what the analysis supports, and scope proportionate to the claimed contribution; venue alignment is not assessed because no review target is bound.
what_triggers_block: The claimed novelty or significance is not demonstrated, for example the gap is not established against existing work or the headline contribution depends on claims the analysis does not support, so that the contribution needs substantial reframing before it could be judged.
what_triggers_warn: The contribution is real but under-articulated or overstated in places, such as implications that stretch beyond the data, significance stated without supporting argument, or novelty that is incremental and not candidly positioned.
what_triggers_fatal: No original contribution can be identified, for example the work duplicates existing findings without acknowledgement or its central contribution rests on a claim that is shown to be unsupported or invalid.
